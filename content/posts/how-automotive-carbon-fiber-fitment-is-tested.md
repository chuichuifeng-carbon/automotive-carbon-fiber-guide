A supplier sends a photograph of a BMW with a carbon front lip installed. The outline looks right and the caption says “perfect fitment.” Before placing a wholesale order, you still need to know which lip was fitted, which bumper was used, what hardware was needed and whether the installer modified anything.

**A useful fitment test identifies the product and vehicle, checks the relevant interfaces and records the work required to achieve the result.** A finished-car photograph is supporting evidence. It is not the whole test.

For an overseas distributor, this distinction affects the retail listing, installation estimate and return risk. The question is whether the evidence supports the application you intend to sell.

## First establish what was tested

Record the product code and revision, then the actual vehicle: chassis, body, production details, bumper or housing and relevant equipment. Include modifications to the interfaces. A test on an aftermarket bumper may be useful for that bumper without establishing compatibility with the original one.

A part number or scan can help identify a starting reference. Neither proves that the finished molded and trimmed component fits. CCF's [vehicle directory](https://ccfrp.com/vehicles/) helps buyers locate applications, but the selected product still needs its own evidence.

For a new project, arrange the vehicle and sample together before the session. Identify missing equipment or other limitations in advance. Testing a diffuser without the intended exhaust fitted leaves a significant part of the application unresolved.

## Geometry checks and vehicle installation answer different questions

A fixture or dimensional check can compare selected features with a defined reference. A vehicle trial can reveal assembly access, interactions with neighboring parts and the real installation sequence. Ask what each check covers.

For example, a hole-position fixture may establish that certain holes meet the production reference. It does not automatically show that a technician can reach them with the undertray in place. Conversely, one successful vehicle installation may not explain how the factory checks the next batch.

| Evidence | Useful for | Limit to keep visible |
|---|---|---|
| Defined dimensions or fixture result | Checking selected features consistently | Only the features and reference actually evaluated |
| Loose-part interface photographs | Reviewing attachment design and supplied components | Cannot demonstrate a completed installation |
| Identified vehicle trial | Checking assembly and relevant adjacent interfaces | Applies to the recorded combination |
| Final installed photograph | Showing overall appearance | May conceal modification, missing fixings or unresolved functions |

Ask for the available evidence for the selected order. CCF's [aero-parts page](https://ccfrp.com/products/aero-parts/) specifically cautions against assuming every component has vehicle-specific test evidence.

## Check how the part locates before tightening it down

For a front lip, review seating across the bumper, both corner returns, section joints and underside fixing access. A sample that only aligns after the fasteners pull it into position needs assessment. The finished photograph alone will not reveal that condition.

For side skirts, check the actual body variant, both ends and the attachment surface. A correct overall length does not establish that the end profiles or mounting locations agree.

For a replacement panel, involve the appropriate technician in the hinge, latch, support and transferred-component review. A panel sitting in an opening is not evidence that its closure and installed operation have been evaluated.

Use the product-specific installation method. Do not borrow fastener lengths, tightening values or hole positions from another part because its title names the same car.

## Measure gaps against agreed locations

“Small gap” and “OEM fit” are difficult to enforce without a reference. Identify where the gap or alignment will be checked, how it will be observed or measured and what acceptance criterion applies.

Use consistent reference points. A dimension measured from a flexible edge or a photograph taken at an angle can be misleading. Photograph the full area as well as the close-up so the reviewer can locate the measurement.

There is no universal millimeter limit suitable for every carbon lip, diffuser, mirror cap and replacement panel. The design and agreed application determine what needs to be controlled. Where the parties use an original component as a comparison, identify that reference and its condition.

For a remote review, distinguish what the images establish from what remains unmeasured. CCF's [remote sample-review guide](https://ccfrp.com/remote-carbon-fiber-sample-review/) helps keep a photographic appearance review from turning into an unsupported fitment approval.

## Diffusers need the exhaust relationship checked

A diffuser can clip into a bumper while leaving an unsuitable relationship to the exhaust. Identify outlet shape, position and projection, plus the exact aftermarket exhaust if present.

Review the complete assembly: center panel, outer sections, surround and any brackets. A missing outer section can look like a geometric problem when the purchase itself was incomplete.

Clearance and heat requirements need the relevant product and vehicle information. A cold static photograph cannot validate every operating condition. Record which questions the session answered and which require further assessment.

Likewise, a wing or spoiler conversion needs its supports, receiving panel and any relevant movement reviewed. Physical seating does not establish an aerodynamic load rating or approval of a changed vehicle function.

## Record every adjustment needed

If installation requires trimming, drilling, additional hardware or removal of existing trim, record it against the proposed instructions. Some modifications may be part of the documented method. Others may indicate a wrong application or a problem requiring correction.

Do not describe both situations simply as “installed successfully.” A shop deciding whether to sell the part needs to know the actual labor and changes involved.

When the cause is unclear, preserve the received condition and report it before irreversible work. CCF's [checks before trimming or drilling](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-parts-not-fitting/) provides a useful escalation route. Wide views, labels and marked contact areas are easier to assess than an isolated close-up labeled “bad fit.”

## Approve the tested revision, not the entire catalog

A sample decision should identify the product, vehicle, findings and remaining issues. If the visible finish is accepted while an exhaust interface is pending, keep those outcomes separate.

CCF's [sample inspection guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-sample-inspection-wholesale-order/) explains how to distinguish observations from acceptance. For fitment, attach the vehicle record and installation findings rather than relying on a general “sample approved” message.

A correction also needs review. If a revised lip has a changed mounting flange, keep the old and new samples identifiable. Approval of the previous visible face does not establish the new interface.

## How does the factory control the next batch?

The vehicle trial provides an application reference; repeat production needs a way to maintain the accepted geometry and contents. Discuss which dimensions, fixtures, attachment features and inspection records are used for the product.

Changes to tooling, trimming, bonded brackets or supplied hardware may affect the installation even when the visible outline remains similar. Agree how proposed changes are communicated before treating a reorder as identical.

This is a separate question from whether the manufacturer can produce one good sample. Keep the accepted reference with your SKU and retail application data so later staff can identify what the evidence actually supports.

## Request evidence for the product you will buy

Send [CCF wholesale](https://ccfrp.com/grow/wholesale/) the vehicle, selected reference, quantity and destination. Ask what fitment records and installation information are available, which configuration was evaluated and what sample review can be arranged.

For new geometry or another application, take the question through [CCF OEM/ODM review](https://ccfrp.com/grow/oem-odm/) before treating it as an existing supported SKU.

A useful request is specific: “Please identify the vehicle and product revision used for the trial, show the mounting interfaces and explain any additional hardware or modification required.” The answer gives your installer a basis for pricing the work and your sales team a defensible application statement.

## Buyer questions

### Does a successful test fit prove all model years fit?

It supports the recorded configuration. Broader coverage needs evidence for the relevant differences; a shared model name is insufficient.

### Is a scan comparison enough?

It can support geometry review, but finished-part trimming, attachments and assembly interactions still need suitable checks.

### Should every part be fitted to a car before dispatch?

Agree a proportionate inspection plan for the product. Vehicle trials and production checks serve different purposes; this guide does not claim every supplier test-fits every shipped unit.

### Can fitment photos support a wholesale order?

Yes, when they identify the relevant product and vehicle and are accompanied by clear installation findings. Ask what remains unverified before relying on them in your customer promise.
