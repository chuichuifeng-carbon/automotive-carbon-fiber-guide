**If a carbon fiber prototype's appearance is accepted but fitment remains unverified, record those as separate decisions and state what work is authorized next.** Do not use a general “approved” message if the supplier could interpret it as permission to begin production. Identify the sample revision, accepted features, open issues, responsible reviewers and the explicit release boundary.

This guide helps aftermarket brands and project buyers prepare a clear operational record. It is not an engineering approval or a substitute for agreed contractual authority. Its purpose is to prevent a positive appearance review from becoming a broader production instruction than the buyer intended.

## Why a favorable comment can create an ambiguous decision

During a remote review, a buyer might send a message saying “looks good, please continue.” The buyer may mean that the finish is acceptable and the next sample can be prepared. The supplier may believe the buyer accepted the product and wants a production batch.

The ambiguity becomes greater when photos, physical samples and vehicle checks refer to different revisions. A polished sample can look satisfactory while its mounting interfaces still need review on the intended vehicle. Conversely, a fitment session might accept the interfaces but leave a visible finish defect unresolved.

CCF's [prototype approval checklist](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-prototype-approval-checklist/) makes the approval scope a useful starting point. Your actual record should connect that scope to a named revision and the work you intend to release.

## Separate the review into decisions with evidence

| Review area | Possible evidence | Decision to record |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Sample reference, file revision, labels and photographs | This is the item being reviewed |
| Appearance | Physical reference, agreed lighting and defect criteria | Accepted features and remaining deviations |
| Fitment | Identified vehicle, mounting method and interface observations | What applications and interfaces were checked |
| Function | Relevant tests or specialist assessment within the agreed scope | What intended-use requirements are supported |
| Packaging | Identified pack configuration and receiving observations | Whether the proposed delivery protection is accepted |
| Production release | Agreed specification, closed conditions and authorized decision-maker | Work, quantity and revision permitted to proceed |

Not every project needs every row in the same form. However, omitting a review should mean “not reviewed” or “not applicable with a reason,” rather than silently treating it as passed.

For a general inspection structure, use CCF's guide to [sample inspection before a wholesale order](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-sample-inspection-wholesale-order/). Adapt the record to a development prototype: a prototype can contain agreed temporary features that must not be carried into production without review.

## Use precise decision states

Three operational states are often enough if their meaning is explicit.

**Accepted within the named scope:** the reviewed features satisfy the recorded criteria. Name the scope, such as visible finish only, and identify any reliance on photographs rather than physical inspection.

**Conditional next-stage release:** a specified activity may proceed while listed conditions remain controlled. State the conditions, permitted activity and any quantity limit. This is not a universal workaround for an unresolved safety or compatibility requirement.

**Hold:** work affected by an unresolved requirement must wait. Define the information or correction needed to reopen the decision and who is responsible for reviewing it.

Avoid a generic green check beside the whole project. The record needs to tell a production planner or a replacement contact what they may actually do.

## Worked example: finish accepted, vehicle review still required

This is an invented teaching example, not a CCF project or a recommendation to begin work under incomplete requirements.

A brand receives prototype revision B for a cosmetic exterior component. A physical review accepts the visible weave and finish against the agreed reference. The buyer has not yet arranged the intended vehicle, and mounting clearances are still unverified. The supplier asks whether it may begin a batch.

An inadequate response is “Sample approved, except we still need to check fitment.” It combines a broad approval with a qualification whose consequence is unclear.

A clearer record looks like this:

| Field | Example decision |
|---|---|
| Reviewed item | Prototype B, identified by sample label and photo record |
| Accepted scope | Visible appearance under the recorded physical review conditions |
| Not yet accepted | Mounting interfaces and clearances on the stated vehicle configuration |
| Next agreed activity | Arrange and conduct the defined test-fit session |
| Production status | Batch production is not authorized by this record |
| Decision owner | Named buyer reviewer with the agreed approval role |
| Reopening condition | Test-fit findings recorded and reviewed against the applicable criteria |

The accepted appearance does not disappear. It becomes a reference for subsequent revisions. If a later correction changes the visible surface, that affected scope needs another review.

The supplier should acknowledge the decision and raise any activity it believes is necessary before the vehicle review. The parties can then discuss it explicitly rather than relying on an ambiguous “continue.”

## Prepare the fitment check as a defined event

Identify the actual vehicle configuration, installed equipment, prior modifications and the component revision. Agree the installation method, personnel, measurement references and photographs needed. List questions that the session cannot answer.

CCF's [guide to planning a test-fit vehicle session](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-prototype-test-fit-vehicle/) helps organize this work. A general appointment with a car is not enough if the correct bumper, mounting arrangement or relevant equipment is missing.

Do not turn an informal visual check into structural or regulatory approval. Where intended use requires engineering assessment, define that work separately with the appropriate specialist. The record should describe what was checked rather than claiming more than the evidence supports.

## Control changes after the decision

A release applies to an identified configuration. If construction, mounting, finish, hardware or another relevant feature changes, review the impact before reusing the old approval.

Some changes may affect only one part of the record. Others may invalidate the basis of several earlier decisions. The correct response is an impact review, not an automatic demand to repeat everything or a blanket assumption that nothing important changed.

CCF's [order-change approval guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-order-change-approval/) is useful when the technical record affects production or dispatch instructions. Keep the commercial order, technical revision and release message consistent.

>

>

>

>
> Work still on hold:
>
> Authorized quantity, if relevant:
>
> Required next evidence and responsible person:
>
> Buyer decision and supplier acknowledgement:

Make the evidence references durable: identify the photographs, measurement record or inspection notes rather than writing “see WhatsApp.” Preserve the decision in the project's normal records so it remains understandable when team members change.

## A short message to accompany the record

If a launch deadline creates pressure to proceed, record the unresolved condition and the decision being requested instead of weakening its description. “Urgent” does not identify an accepted deviation, an authorized quantity or a person who can release work. Ask the responsible reviewer to decide within the agreed process. Where no acceptable route is available, a hold is a meaningful project outcome: it keeps the record accurate and prevents additional work from being based on an assumption. Schedule consequences and commercial options can then be discussed as separate, visible decisions.

> We accept the appearance of [sample/revision] within the attached review scope. Fitment remains unverified for [configuration]. This message authorizes [specific next activity] only and does not authorize batch production. Please acknowledge the scope and identify any proposed change to the reviewed revision before proceeding. We will review the next-stage findings against [agreed criteria/reference].

Adjust the wording to the actual authority and agreement. A template cannot grant authority that the sender does not have or resolve commercial terms that the parties have not discussed.

## Questions to answer before the next release

Can the receiving team identify the exact sample? Does everyone understand which features are accepted? Are unresolved issues connected to a responsible person and next evidence? Does the proposed production revision match the accepted scope? Is the permitted next activity unmistakable?

If the answer to one of those questions is unclear, clarify the record before treating the project as ready.
