**Start wholesale sourcing with a focused vehicle and SKU shortlist, verified samples and a realistic landed-cost model.** A catalog price is only one input. Decide how the products will be installed, sold, replenished and supported before increasing inventory.

## Choose the supply arrangement

| Arrangement | Suitable starting question | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Stocked inventory | Which proven SKUs justify capital and shelf space? | Demand, packing, receiving checks and replenishment |
| Purchase against customer orders | Can the promised customer delivery be supported? | Availability, order confirmation and realistic timing |
| Hybrid inventory | Which products need stock and which can wait? | Separate service promises for each group |
| Private label | What changes beyond product selection? | Branding, packaging minimums and approval |
| Custom development | Is a catalog product insufficient? | Tooling, sample and production release process |

Do not advertise stock or delivery dates based on an unconfirmed supplier estimate. Catalog listings, production availability and finished inventory are different facts.

## Build the first-order shortlist

Choose a customer segment, vehicle configuration and manageable set of components. For each SKU record construction, finish, replacement/overlay design, kit contents, fitment evidence, unit of measure and installation requirements.

Avoid assuming one component fits every trim or body style in a vehicle family. For a kit, itemize every part and exclusion. Check packaging dimensions early: long or bulky exterior parts can change the economics of a small shipment.

## Calculate contribution, not just markup

**Contribution per sold unit = net sales revenue − delivered product cost − variable selling, fulfillment and after-sales costs.** Keep fixed overhead and tax treatment separate in your business model.

Illustrative example: a $500 sale minus $280 delivered product cost, $50 selling fees, $30 fulfillment and $20 returns allowance leaves $120 contribution before fixed overhead and tax. These are example inputs, not market prices or a return forecast. Replace them with your own data and avoid counting the same cost twice.

## Qualify before a larger purchase

1. [Compare supplier offers]({{< relref "posts/how-to-choose-a-carbon-fiber-parts-supplier.md" >}}) on the same specification and delivery basis.
2. [Inspect the sample]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}) and document revision and fitment limitations.
3. Confirm [MOQ, tooling charges and quantity breaks]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-moq-guide.md" >}}).
4. Agree packing, inspection, claims terms and shipping documents.
5. Plan a limited first order and record receiving results.

## Reorder using actual evidence

Track sell-through by SKU, installation feedback, returns, supplier delivery and complete margin. Reorder points should reflect demand during replenishment plus a justified buffer; promotional spikes and long-tail variants need separate judgment.

Maintain an approved SKU record when tooling, construction or packaging changes. A successful first shipment does not verify all future revisions.
