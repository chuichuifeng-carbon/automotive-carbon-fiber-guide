**Dry carbon usually means a prepreg-based part; wet carbon usually means resin is introduced during layup or infusion.** These aftermarket labels do not, by themselves, prove full-carbon construction, autoclave curing, low weight, accurate fitment or suitability for structural use. Request the process and finished-part specification for the exact SKU.

## Process, construction and finish are different specifications

| Term | What it describes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Prepreg / dry carbon | Reinforcement supplied with a controlled resin system | Whether a particular part uses autoclave or another approved cure process |
| Wet layup | Liquid resin applied to reinforcement during manufacture | The finished laminate's strength or quality without supporting evidence |
| Vacuum infusion | Resin drawn through reinforcement under vacuum | A guaranteed weight, finish or service temperature |
| Full carbon / hybrid / overlay | The component's material construction | Whether it replaces an original part or bonds over one |
| Gloss / matte / forged appearance | Surface appearance or material pattern | The manufacturing process or structural capability |

Ask separately about reinforcement, backing, core, resin, cure method, coating and installation. A visible carbon surface may conceal a hybrid backing. Read CCF's [full-carbon versus fiberglass-backed parts comparison](https://ccfrp.com/full-carbon-vs-fiberglass-backed-carbon-parts/) when comparing quotations.

## Why process alone cannot predict weight or fitment

Prepreg can support controlled resin content and repeatable layup. Actual results depend on the laminate design, tooling, cure cycle, trimming and inspection. Cure temperatures and pressures are resin- and process-specific; there is no single recipe for every automotive part.

Likewise, accurate fitment depends on geometry and mounting interfaces. A well-controlled wet-process part may fit better than an incorrectly tooled prepreg part. Require test-fit records for the exact vehicle configuration rather than treating “dry carbon” as a fitment guarantee.

For weight claims, compare complete assemblies with equivalent hardware and coatings. An overlay adds material to an existing part; it should not be marketed as a weight-saving replacement. Record the original component weight, replacement weight, scale, configuration and exclusions. Do not apply a generic percentage saving to every SKU.

## Choose by intended application

| Buyer requirement | Evidence to request before approval |
|---|---|
| Visible exterior trim | Agreed surface standard, coating information, edge finish and vehicle fitment |
| Interior overlay | Adhesive method, clearance and compatibility with controls and existing trim |
| Replacement hood or trunk | Latch, hinge and support interfaces; installation instructions and application-specific engineering evidence |
| Wing or load-bearing assembly | Mounting design, load assumptions and relevant test evidence |
| Repeat wholesale supply | Approved reference sample, revision control and inspection records |

Do not infer road legality, crash performance or load capacity from a material name. For safety-relevant components, have a qualified specialist review the intended application and required evidence before release.

## A specification buyers can send

> Please confirm whether [SKU] is a replacement or overlay; its reinforcement and backing materials; prepreg/wet-layup/infusion process; cure method; finished weight and measurement scope; weave and finish; mounting hardware; and verified vehicle applications. Please separate measured results from estimates and identify available test-fit or engineering records.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I identify prepreg from gloss, weight or a photograph?

Not reliably. Gloss is a coating choice, and weight depends on design and backing. Use manufacturing records and a written specification; a physical sample is still needed for fitment and appearance approval.

### Is forged carbon stronger than woven carbon?

Appearance alone does not establish a mechanical advantage. Fiber architecture, resin, geometry and process affect performance. Ask what construction is actually supplied and what tests support the intended use.

### Should every buyer choose dry carbon?

Choose the construction that meets your documented requirements at an acceptable total cost. Compare like-for-like specifications, then use the [sample inspection checklist]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}) to qualify the product.
