**Ask for order-specific evidence before paying for a carbon fiber order.** A factory label, polished quotation or low price cannot replace documented identity, specifications and acceptance terms. These seven questions help buyers turn a conversation into a reviewable purchasing record.

## 1. Who is contracting, manufacturing and receiving payment?

> Please identify the legal seller, manufacturing site, any subcontracted operations and the beneficiary on the invoice. What company and factory verification information is available?

Reconcile any different entities before payment. A head office can legitimately have a different address from a production base. A trading supplier can be useful if its role, quality responsibilities and production access are transparent. A refused video call alone does not prove fraud; ask for another practical verification method.

## 2. How are tooling and product revisions controlled?

> Is this existing tooling or a development project? Please confirm the tooling reference, current revision, maintenance responsibility, permitted use and change-approval process.

For buyer-funded tooling, document ownership, storage, exclusivity if any, and release terms. A specific machine type does not guarantee component accuracy. Request inspection evidence for the features that affect installation. CCF's [tooling and mold ownership questions](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-tooling-mold-ownership-questions/) can help prepare the project brief.

## 3. What exactly has been test-fitted?

> Which vehicle configuration was used to verify this SKU? Please provide the revision, installation method, photographs, measured interfaces and any unverified applications.

Vehicle availability may require buyer-side or third-party test fitting during development. Agree responsibility, cost and release conditions instead of assuming every supplier owns every vehicle. Separate verified applications from proposed compatibility.

## 4. What does the inspection plan actually check?

> Please propose the inspection scope, lot definition, sample selection, defect classes, acceptance criteria and actions after failure. Can we agree independent inspection access before shipment?

**AQL 2.5 does not mean that every shipment may contain 2.5% defective parts.** Acceptance sampling uses a defined plan and acceptance/rejection counts; [ISO's overview of ISO 2859-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/2859-1) explains the role of AQL-indexed sampling. Agree the complete plan with a qualified inspector; do not interpret the AQL number as a batch warranty. Critical features need their own agreed controls.

Use the [sample inspection checklist]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}) to define what must be checked, then agree how bulk conformity will be assessed.

## 5. What milestones trigger payment?

> Please state deposit and balance terms, the evidence required at each milestone, cancellation provisions and the process for verifying a changed bank account.

No payment split proves a supplier is reliable. Samples and stock orders can have different terms from custom tooling. Review your exposure, payment protections and inspection rights together. Verify unexpected beneficiary changes through a previously established contact channel.

## 6. How will the exact part be packed?

> Please provide the proposed packaging, component protection, packed dimensions and weight. Who handles freight damage, insurance and claims evidence?

Long side skirts, sharp edges and painted surfaces need different protection. Desiccant alone cannot guarantee coating performance or prevent every transit defect. Approve packaging for the product and route, including branding and labels if relevant.

## 7. What happens when an order does not conform?

> Please confirm the claim window, required evidence, assessment process, remedies and responsibility for replacement freight, return freight and approved rework.

Do not assume installation labor, consequential losses or return transport are covered. Put agreed remedies on the order or contract and retain the applicable revision.

## Decision record before placing an order

| Outcome | Purchasing action |
|---|---|
| Evidence supports the defined requirements | Proceed to the agreed sample or next milestone |
| Information is missing but can be obtained | Hold the affected decision and request the missing record |
| Statements conflict with documents or the sample | Resolve the discrepancy before increasing exposure |
| The offer cannot meet a required condition | Change the scope or choose another offer |

## Frequently asked questions

### Must a reliable supplier accept 30/70 payment terms?

No. Terms depend on the order, credit history and commercial arrangement. Evaluate payment exposure and verification together rather than using one percentage as a reliability test.

### Is a quality-management certificate enough?

No. Verify its scope and validity, then inspect the actual product against the agreed specification. A certificate is one part of supplier review.

### Can repeat orders skip qualification?

Retain controls for new SKUs, changed tooling, construction, sites or packaging. Repeat supply history is useful evidence, but it does not verify an untested revision.
