**Compare carbon fiber parts by total cost per saleable installation, not factory price alone.** A cheaper component can become more expensive if it needs unplanned fitting work, return freight or replacement. A higher price, however, is not proof of better fitment: compare evidence and agreed specifications.

## What a fitment problem can involve

Common issues to check include mounting-point mismatch, uneven contact surfaces, interference with adjacent components, missing hardware and unclear installation instructions. A mismatch can come from the part, a wrong application, prior vehicle repairs, aftermarket modifications or an installation method that differs from the specification.

Record the vehicle configuration and actual measurements before assigning responsibility. Use the [sample inspection checklist]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}) to identify these issues before buying a batch.

## Illustrative cost comparison

The figures below are invented examples to demonstrate a calculation. They are not CCF prices, market labor rates or predicted defect rates. Replace them with your own quotations and experience.

| Cost per installation | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Delivered component cost | $200 | $260 |
| Planned installation: 1 hour at $80/hour | $80 | $80 |
| Unplanned rework | 1.5 hours × $80 = $120 | $0 |
| Additional consumables | $15 | $0 |
| Total before any return | **$415** | **$340** |

Offer A is $60 cheaper to buy but $75 more expensive in this scenario. If the rework is not authorized, cannot achieve the agreed result or creates a safety concern, a return or replacement may be the appropriate outcome instead.

For a batch, model expected after-sales cost separately:

**Expected cost per sold unit = delivered unit cost + normal installation cost + expected rework cost + expected returns/replacement cost.**

Use your observed rates, state assumptions and avoid counting the same failure in both rework and returns. Also track cash tied up in unsaleable inventory.

## Specify fitment before paying for a sample

| Requirement | What to record |
|---|---|
| Vehicle application | Model, generation, body style, production date and relevant options |
| Component identity | SKU, revision, replacement or overlay, kit contents |
| Installation | Allowed drilling, bonding, trimming and supplied hardware |
| Interfaces | Mounting locations, contact areas, clearances and agreed measuring points |
| Appearance | Gap/alignment acceptance criteria for this component |
| Verification | Test-fit configuration, photos, installer feedback and release status |
| Claims | Evidence, timing, assessment and responsibility for approved remedies |

There is no universal “±1 mm” mounting tolerance suitable for every carbon fiber product. Agree measurable, component-specific criteria and a practical inspection method.

## If a delivered part does not fit

1. Stop installation before forcing, cutting or bonding the part.
2. Check the order, SKU and vehicle configuration, including existing modifications.
3. Photograph the unmodified component, label, mounting points and measured mismatch.
4. Ask the supplier to review the evidence against the agreed application and installation method.
5. Obtain written approval before rework if you expect reimbursement or warranty support.
6. Isolate affected inventory while determining whether the issue is individual or shared by the batch.

Do not assume the supplier covers labor, lost sales or return freight. Confirm those terms before ordering; keep the quote and approval record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is any trimming evidence of a defective part?

Not necessarily. Some designs require disclosed fitting work. The issue is whether the supplied part and required installation match the agreement and intended application. Undisclosed or unsafe modifications should be resolved before proceeding.

### Does dry carbon guarantee better fitment?

No. Tooling, geometry, trimming and verification determine fitment. The [manufacturing comparison]({{< relref "posts/dry-vs-wet-carbon-fiber.md" >}}) explains why process labels cannot replace application evidence.

### How do I compare suppliers without knowing their return rates?

Use a controlled sample evaluation and a limited trial order. Record your own installation time, defects and claims outcomes rather than accepting unsupported “zero returns” promises.
