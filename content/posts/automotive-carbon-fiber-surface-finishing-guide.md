A gloss carbon front lip can look flawless under showroom lights and still look wrong beside the side skirts the customer already owns. The reflections may be sharper, the weave may run in a different direction, or the clear coat may look cloudy at the corners. None of those differences is resolved by ordering another part marked “gloss 2x2.”

**Specify the carbon pattern, coating appearance and acceptance standard separately.** For a wholesale order, approve a physical reference for the visible surfaces, check each part in a matching set, and agree what the factory should do if a later batch differs.

## Gloss or matte: choose for the whole car

Gloss produces sharper reflections. That can emphasize the depth of visible carbon, but reflections also make waviness and polishing marks easier to see from some angles. Matte produces more diffuse reflections; uneven sheen or a shiny patch can stand out even where the weave itself looks consistent.

A customer adding a rear diffuser to an existing gloss package is making a different choice from one ordering a new matte package throughout. For the first job, ask to see the existing finish in person where practical. For the second, compare the proposed pieces together before approval. Two suppliers can both write “matte” and deliver noticeably different sheen.

Satin needs the same treatment. It is a useful description in a conversation, but not a repeatable acceptance limit by itself. If gloss is measured, agree on the instrument geometry, measurement locations and acceptable variation. Curved components may need a separate representative reference rather than a reading taken wherever the instrument happens to sit flat.

## Clear coat and carbon construction answer different questions

A visible woven face tells you little about the backing or cure process. Both prepreg and non-prepreg parts can be sold with gloss or matte finishes. A low-gloss part is not inherently dry carbon, and a deep gloss is not proof of wet layup. See [CCF's dry versus wet carbon comparison](https://ccfrp.com/dry-vs-wet-carbon-fiber-tuning-shops/) for the construction side of the quotation.

Ask what surface system the finished component has and whether it is supplied ready to install or requires local finishing. Keep that answer separate from the laminate description. If a shop plans to apply film or another coating, review compatibility with the actual surface and the relevant provider's instructions before promising the result.

## Partially painted carbon: draw the line before quoting

For a hood with exposed carbon around a vent, a finish description needs to identify the boundaries. Which face stays exposed? Do the painted areas extend around the edge returns? Is the underside included? Annotate a photo or drawing and keep it with the approved revision.

If paint matching happens locally, identify what arrives from the factory and what the local body shop is expected to do. A lower factory price can simply mean more preparation and finishing after delivery. Use [CCF's materials and finish resources](https://ccfrp.com/category/materials-comparisons/) to distinguish exposed carbon and painted zones before comparing offers.

## Inspect the surface before fitting or polishing

Start with a clean sample and compare it with the approved reference under consistent lighting. View the whole component at normal installed viewing distance, then look closely at any disputed area. For a pair of mirror caps or side skirts, place both sides in comparable orientations. A reflection can make one side appear different when the pieces are held at different angles.

| What you see | What to record before deciding |
|---|---|
| Pinholes or small pits | Location, visibility and whether they are isolated or repeated |
| Haze or a cloudy patch | Lighting angle, area affected and comparison with the reference |
| Uneven gloss or polishing marks | Whether the change is visible in the installed position and across a pair |
| Weave distortion near a return | Where the pattern changes and whether the approved sample shows the same transition |
| Lifting, peeling or a crack | Edge and close-up photos, handling history and technical assessment before corrective work |

Record the observation separately from the suspected cause. A photograph of a pit does not prove a cure problem, and polishing a disputed area can remove evidence of the original condition. For a matte part, do not assume a gloss-polishing method will preserve its appearance. Follow the guidance for the actual coating; [CCF's care and handover guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-care-instructions/) explains what information to pass on to customers.

## Ask what a UV claim actually covers

A clear-coat weathering report is useful only when the specimen and conditions are identified. Was the specimen a flat coating coupon or a finished carbon component? Which coating and substrate were used? What changed during exposure, and what acceptance limit was applied?

A test-hour figure is not a direct conversion to years outdoors. For an exterior lip, wing or mirror cap, ask how the tested system relates to the supplied product. [CCF's guide to UV and clear-coat reports](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-uv-resistance-test-report/) shows how to review that connection without making a service-life promise the report cannot support.

## Turn one good sample into a repeatable order

An approved sample is valuable when both sides know what it represents. Record the product revision, finish, inspection lighting and visible zones. Photograph the sample before installation, including the edges and the pair together where applicable. Keep any agreed exceptions with the sample record.

For a repeat order, ask whether the coating, finishing process or appearance reference changed. Review representative pieces where practical, rather than assuming a carefully selected sample describes the whole shipment. CCF's [manufacturer verification guide](https://ccfrp.com/how-to-find-reliable-automotive-carbon-fiber-manufacturer/) covers why production consistency deserves its own review.

Keep fitment separate. A flawless finish does not approve the mounting geometry, and a part that fits can still fail an agreed appearance requirement. Document both before accepting a wider release.

## What to put on the purchase order

A useful finish line reads: “Exterior visible faces to match approved sample [reference]; [gloss/matte] as shown; [weave appearance]; painted zones and returns per annotated drawing [revision]. Inspect paired pieces together. Obtain approval before any coating or appearance change.”

Send the selected part references, vehicle application and expected quantities through [CCF wholesale](https://ccfrp.com/grow/wholesale/) when requesting a sample. Include photos of the customer's existing carbon if the job involves matching it. The point of the sample is to settle what the buyer will receive and what the shop can confidently promise on the next order.
