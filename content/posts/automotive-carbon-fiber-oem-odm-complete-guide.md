**Begin a custom carbon fiber project with a feasibility brief and written approval gates.** Separate development costs from production pricing, and define who designs, verifies and approves each deliverable. OEM and ODM labels vary; the agreement controls the actual scope.

## Catalog, private label or new development?

| Route | Main buyer decision | Terms to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Existing catalog part | Does a verified application meet the need? | Availability, specification and sample acceptance |
| Private label | What branding or packing changes are needed? | Artwork, permissions, packaging MOQ and approvals |
| Buyer-defined design | Is the supplied design ready for manufacture? | File revision, rights, design responsibility and verification |
| Supplier-supported development | What engineering work is required? | Deliverables, ownership, assumptions and approval gates |

A reference photo communicates appearance, but may not establish dimensions, mounting geometry or rights to reproduce a design. Record missing inputs before treating a quotation as a final production commitment.

## Five stages and release evidence

| Stage | Required output before proceeding |
|---|---|
| Project review | Vehicle, intended use, product scope, references and unknowns |
| Feasibility | Proposed construction, development route, interfaces, verification and costs |
| Tooling and prototype | Identified tooling and physical sample with revision and deviations |
| Sample approval | Inspection results, concessions, corrections and explicit next-stage authorization |
| Production | Approved specification, quantity, inspection plan, packing and commercial terms |

CCF's [official OEM/ODM page](https://ccfrp.com/grow/oem-odm/) describes its service workflow. Confirm the applicable deliverables for your project rather than assuming a general service page is a contract.

## Tooling terms to settle before payment

- Ownership of molds, drawings, CAD and manufacturing data.
- Permitted use, exclusivity if agreed, and any limitations.
- Initial scope, trial parts, revisions and additional charges.
- Maintenance, storage duration and responsibility for damage.
- Access, transfer/release conditions and the outcome if development stops.

A tooling fee alone does not establish ownership or exclusivity. Record the agreement and its relationship to production pricing.

## Prototype approval is a decision, not a compliment

Specify the test-fit vehicle or fixture, inspection method and acceptance criteria. Agree whether the buyer, supplier or a third party provides the installation environment. Include mounting, clearances, appearance, hardware and relevant functional/engineering requirements.

Use the [sample inspection checklist]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}) and CCF's [prototype approval record guidance](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-prototype-approval-checklist/). Keep appearance approval separate from structural validation and market approval.

## RFQ template

> Application/intended use: [details]. Reference and rights: [CAD, sample or photos]. Interfaces: [mounts and clearances]. Construction/finish: [requirements]. Volume: [prototype, first order, forecast]. Verification: [vehicle/fixture access and required evidence]. Please separate feasibility, tooling, prototypes, revisions, testing, production and packaging costs. Identify assumptions, exclusions and release conditions.

Discuss confidentiality before sending sensitive files. Quote dates should identify their trigger: approved scope, tooling authorization, sample release or production deposit, as applicable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can development begin without CAD?

An initial discussion can start from references or an idea. Confirm what measurements, design work and approvals are needed before tooling.

### Can sample and bulk production run together?

Only with explicit authorization defining the permitted work and accepted risk. Otherwise release production after the relevant sample conditions are met.

### Does a new mold guarantee a unique product?

No. Design rights, tooling ownership and exclusivity are separate matters. Agree them explicitly if they affect your business case.

### What should be reconfirmed after a design change?

Update the revision, affected interfaces, cost, timing and verification plan. Reapprove the changed scope before the next release.
