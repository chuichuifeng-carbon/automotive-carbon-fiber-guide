An Audi RS6 customer may want a restrained carbon lip and roof spoiler, or a complete lower-body package with a different diffuser. A distributor can find photographs for both in minutes. Turning those images into a saleable order takes more work: the generation, current bumper, exhaust and supplied pieces must agree.

**Choose Audi RS6 carbon fiber parts around a verified vehicle application and a complete installation scope.** For a first wholesale order, a few products your shop can identify and fit are more useful than several similar styles with uncertain contents. The right range comes from your customer inquiries and sample results, not from a supplier's most dramatic build photograph.

## Put the generation on the purchase order

Do not order against “RS6 Avant” alone. Record the generation, production details and actual body configuration, then identify the existing bumper and relevant modifications. C7 and C8 should be separate application records; an RS6 badge does not make their exterior components interchangeable.

Within a generation, distinguish the vehicle as built from the vehicle now in the workshop. A replacement bumper, aftermarket exhaust or earlier carbon installation can change the interface. Photograph those areas before requesting the part.

Use [CCF's vehicle directory](https://ccfrp.com/vehicles/) to start the application inquiry. Its published coverage is a route to product identification, not proof that a particular RS6 component is currently stocked. Ask for the actual reference and supported application before listing it for sale.

Registration year alone can be a poor way to resolve production changes across markets. Where the application is uncertain, obtain appropriate vehicle-specific information and have the supplier identify the version it has evaluated.

## Select parts by the job, rather than by a complete-car image

A styling photograph can contain products from several sources. It can also hide mounting parts and retained original trim. Break the intended result into components before discussing a package discount.

| Product | Customer choice | Purchasing issue that affects the finished job |
|---|---|---|
| Front lip | Center profile, corner shape and projection | Bumper seating, underside fixings and supplied sections |
| Side skirts | Visible extension and end treatment | Body application, left/right contents and mounting surface |
| Rear diffuser | Lower-rear shape and fin design | Exhaust openings, surrounding trim and brackets |
| Roof spoiler | Rear roofline treatment | Contact surface, retained spoiler and attachment method |
| Mirror caps | Carbon accent with cover or replacement structure | Current housing, installation access and attachment features |

These are selection categories, not evidence that every version is available from CCF. Send the shortlist for product-specific review. The main-site [RS6 fitment resource](https://ccfrp.com/audi-rs6-carbon-fiber-parts-fitment/) is a connected reference for that discussion.

## Front lips: the underside decides the installation

A front lip's outline is the easy part to sell. Before quoting the job, show the technician the attachment side. Establish where the lip seats against the current bumper, which original trim remains and how the supplied fixing arrangement is accessed.

For a multi-section design, inspect the joints as well as the individual pieces. Parts that look acceptable separately may leave uneven transitions when offered up as an assembly. For a full-width lip, check seating across the entire span rather than fastening one corner and pulling the rest into position.

Ask whether hardware is included and whether drilling forms part of the product-specific method. An installation video for another RS6 lip cannot establish screw locations, fastener lengths or tightening requirements for yours.

If the customer has lowered the car, assess the proposed projection and clearance on that configuration. A photograph taken on another suspension setup cannot answer whether the lip suits daily access to a particular driveway. Nor does an aggressive shape establish a measured aerodynamic benefit.

## Side skirts: sell a defined pair

Write the quoted unit explicitly: one vehicle-left and one vehicle-right component, where that is the accepted supply arrangement. Confirm whether the product extends the existing sill, covers a surface or replaces a section. Each arrangement needs different installation information.

Request end views and the complete underside. Overall length helps identify a mismatch but does not prove that the end profiles, seating and fixing locations agree with the vehicle.

For a sample pair, compare appearance under the same lighting and preserve the side labels through inspection. If several versions will share your warehouse, keep the supplier reference and application on each inner package. Once a long carton is opened, “Audi carbon skirts” is not enough identification for the next customer order.

Spare-side support is a separate commercial question. A pair packed as two pieces does not mean the supplier sells one side, or that a later revision will match an older installation.

## Rear diffusers: start with the exhaust already fitted

Obtain a straight rear photograph and accessible views of the outlet area. If the exhaust has changed, collect its reference and relevant tip geometry. Describing the look of the outlets is not a substitute for their actual position and dimensions.

Identify the main diffuser, corner pieces, surrounds and mounting parts in the quotation. Exhaust components shown on the demonstration car should remain outside the purchase unless specifically itemized.

The installer needs the applicable clearance and heat-exposure requirements for the real combination. A cold photograph cannot demonstrate suitability during operation. If a required piece or interface remains unresolved, leave the job price provisional rather than entering zero for an unknown cost.

When a sample seats against the bumper but the outlets do not align, investigate both the selected diffuser and the installed exhaust position. Trimming the carbon immediately may destroy useful evidence of a wrong variant or another installation problem.

## Roof spoilers: identify what sits above the rear glass

For an Avant inquiry, locate the exact piece in the reference photograph. Is it a cover over an existing spoiler, an extension, or a replacement component? Ask for the loose part's contact side and an explanation of what remains on the car.

CCF has a dedicated [RS6 roof-spoiler selection article](https://ccfrp.com/audi-rs6-carbon-fiber-roof-spoilers-choosing-the-correct-rear-piece/). Use the selected reference in the inquiry instead of assuming every rear carbon piece in a vehicle photograph belongs to one kit.

During the sample review, inspect the perimeter, contact area and the relationship to adjacent glass and trim. Where the product sits near lighting, washer equipment or another moving component, the installer should review those interfaces under the applicable instructions.

If the original component must be removed, include that work and surface preparation in the labor quote. A decorative extension and a replacement should not inherit the same installation allowance simply because both are called spoilers.

## Carbon construction and finish are separate purchases

A visible twill pattern cannot tell a buyer whether the product has fiberglass backing, a bonded carrier or another hidden structure. Request the construction for the specific SKU, especially when comparing a dry-carbon offer with a differently described alternative.

Gloss and matte describe appearance. They do not establish the manufacturing process or how much the complete assembly weighs. If weight matters, compare equivalent supplied units with the same hardware scope; do not borrow a claimed saving from a different part.

For a lip-and-skirt package, inspect the pieces together before promising a matched result. Look at the surface finish, exposed edges and pattern around corners. Existing carbon on the customer's car may come from another source and should be compared against an actual sample or agreed reference.

## Packaging can change which products make sense to stock

A mirror-cap pair and a long front lip may each count as one order line, but they do not consume equivalent shipping space. Obtain the proposed carton dimensions, gross weight and packing arrangement for the actual mix.

For long or projecting pieces, discuss support at the ends, protected visible edges and separation between components. Where a spoiler or diffuser contains several pieces, the receiving team needs a carton-to-component map so an incomplete delivery is recognizable.

Request a freight quote against that packing proposal. Equal freight allocation per SKU can conceal how much of the shipment is attributable to bulky parts. Apply the forwarder's actual charging basis rather than assuming a quantity discount automatically improves the delivered margin.

Before increasing the first order, inspect how the sample arrives. Photograph the unopened carton, internal support and product labels. CCF's [dealer packing-list guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-packing-list-dealer-checklist/) covers the document side; the physical shipment shows whether the protection works for your handling chain.

## Evaluate the supplier with one traceable sample

Choose a sample for an application you can genuinely inspect. Record the vehicle, reference, supplied contents and installation outcome. An accepted mirror cap cannot qualify the fit of a diffuser from the same factory.

Use [CCF's sample-inspection resource](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-sample-inspection-wholesale-order/) to prepare the receiving review. Add the product-specific interfaces above and involve the technician before permanent alteration. Report any mismatch with labels and clear views while the original condition is still visible.

For repeat supply, retain the accepted version and ask how changes to tooling, construction, finish or contents will be communicated. A supplier comparison becomes useful when it covers the actual product you will receive, including its installation and after-sales work.

## Request the RS6 catalog and wholesale quotation

Send [CCF wholesale](https://ccfrp.com/grow/wholesale/) the generation, vehicle details, current photographs and separate product lines. Include finish, quantities and destination. Ask for available references, sample terms, complete contents, installation information and packing for those items.

If the desired part requires new geometry rather than an existing product, use [CCF OEM/ODM review](https://ccfrp.com/grow/oem-odm/) and separate development from production pricing. A buyer's reference photograph does not establish ready-made supply.

For a first stock range, give priority to verified applications supported by your own inquiries. Keep alternative styles and larger conversions available by quotation until demand, fitting and freight justify stock. That gives the sales team fewer ambiguous products and a clearer answer when a customer asks exactly what is included.

## Audi RS6 buyer questions

### Can RS7 carbon parts be sold for an RS6?

Only where the individual product has supporting application evidence. Related model names or similar front-end photographs do not establish interchangeability for every part.

### Does a carbon diffuser include exhaust tips?

Only when the quotation itemizes them. Identify the diffuser and exhaust scope separately before pricing the finished job.

### Can I order a complete carbon package?

Request an itemized proposal for the actual car. A package name or demonstration image does not define its contents, availability or installation requirements.
