**Verify who makes the part, which process they control and what evidence applies to your exact order.** A manufacturer label is a starting point, not a substitute for a qualified sample or a written specification. This guide focuses on production verification; use our [supplier comparison guide]({{< relref "posts/how-to-choose-a-carbon-fiber-parts-supplier.md" >}}) to choose between qualified offers.

## A manufacturer verification file

| Question | Evidence to request | What remains to check |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the legal seller? | Company identity and quotation details | Contracting entity and payment beneficiary agree or are explained |
| Where is production performed? | Site information and available visit, audit or remote-review arrangements | Which operations are in-house and subcontracted |
| Who controls the tooling? | Tooling reference, revision and maintenance responsibility | Permissions and ownership for buyer-funded tooling |
| How is the part manufactured? | Written construction and process statement | Exact material, backing, finish and component scope |
| How is fitment verified? | SKU-specific vehicle and installation records | Whether your configuration is covered |
| How is the batch checked? | Inspection plan and example records | Features, selection method, criteria and corrective action |

A registered office can differ from the factory address. A supplier may subcontract a specialist operation without being unsuitable. Ask who remains accountable for the result and how changes are controlled.

## Match the evidence to the product

A factory tour shows a production environment; it does not demonstrate every claim in a catalog. A quality-system certificate has a company/site/scope and validity period. A product test report concerns the evaluated specimen and test conditions. Review each for relevance rather than treating a document logo as blanket approval.

For CCF, start with the [official company and factory page on ccfrp.com](https://ccfrp.com/about/). Request the information needed for your order and apply the same checks you would to any other candidate.

## Verify fitment and construction before scaling

Specify the vehicle configuration, SKU revision, replacement-versus-overlay design, process, backing, finish and hardware. Ask what was physically test-fitted and what is only proposed compatibility.

Order a sample using an agreed [inspection checklist]({{< relref "posts/carbon-fiber-sample-evaluation.md" >}}). Retain the reference revision and separate sample acceptance from bulk release. For custom tooling, define who provides the vehicle or fixture and who funds corrections.

## Copyable verification request

> We are evaluating [SKU/project]. Please confirm the legal seller, production site, operations you control and any subcontracted steps. What company verification, tooling/revision records, construction information and test-fit evidence are available for this exact part? Please identify anything not yet verified and propose a sample and inspection plan.

A specific CNC machine, autoclave or inspection instrument is not sufficient evidence of finished-part quality. Focus on the controlled process and measurements relevant to the application.

## When to hold the decision

Hold payment or increased exposure if seller/payment identities conflict, the sample differs from the specification, fitment claims exceed evidence or responsibilities cannot be documented. Resolve the discrepancy rather than counting generic positive answers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Must I buy from a factory rather than a trading supplier?

Choose the arrangement that meets your requirements. A trading supplier may coordinate multiple sources; a factory may provide closer process access. Transparency, accountability and product evidence matter in either case.

### Is a visit necessary for every purchase?

Use a verification level proportionate to the order and risk. High-value tooling or safety-relevant applications may justify more review than a catalog sample. Agree practical alternatives if access is restricted.

### What should be rechecked on repeat orders?

Review changes to tooling, site, construction, packaging and specifications. Prior success with one SKU does not verify another application.
