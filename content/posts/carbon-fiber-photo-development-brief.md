A tuning brand sends a photograph of an aggressive front splitter and asks a carbon fiber manufacturer for a tooling price. The factory can see the styling, but not the bumper contact surface, undertray fixings or hidden brackets. If those details remain unresolved, the initial quote is pricing a development assumption rather than a finished product.

**For a custom carbon fiber front lip, separate the visible shape from the vehicle interface.** The photograph helps explain the look. A verified bumper reference, suitable geometry and an agreed mounting concept allow the manufacturer to assess how to make and attach it. Those are different inputs, and they lead to different costs.

## Existing catalog lip or a new OEM/ODM project?

Before paying for tooling, compare the reference with existing products. A request for a more pronounced corner, a different center profile or a new surface pattern may lead to very different work. The commercial routes are not interchangeable:

- An existing catalog front lip uses an already identified product and its confirmed application.
- A finish or branding request changes a defined existing product, subject to the manufacturer's review.
- A geometry change may require engineering, new tooling and another fitment sample.
- A new design requires the styling and vehicle interfaces to be developed together.

Use [CCF's front-lip product category](https://ccfrp.com/category/product-catalog/catalog-front-lips/) to identify available outlines before assuming a new mold is needed. An existing product that looks similar is a candidate for review, not proof that it matches your vehicle or can accept the requested alteration.

The difference matters when comparing “OEM carbon fiber” quotations. In aftermarket inquiries, OEM can refer to custom manufacturing, original-equipment appearance or a seller's marketing language. State whether you want your own design manufactured, an existing part with branding, or an aftermarket part matching an original mounting interface.

## Send the actual bumper configuration, not just the model badge

For a BMW project, identify the chassis, model year and actual front bumper. Photograph any previously drilled holes, aftermarket vents, existing splitter or undertray changes that affect the interface. A G80 M3 reference should not silently become a G20 3 Series requirement during the discussion.

The bumper and neighboring components form a package. The manufacturer needs to know where the lip seats, what fasteners are accessible, and which original trim remains. If the car will receive a replacement bumper later, use the intended final configuration as the development reference. Scanning today's bumper will not resolve tomorrow's different mounting surface.

Provide an untouched reference where possible. If the only available bumper is damaged, repaired or modified, mark those regions explicitly. Otherwise, the reconstruction can reproduce an unintended distortion as if it were design intent.

## A 3D scan must include the surfaces that control fitment

An exterior-only scan may communicate the lower outline beautifully while missing the underside where the lip attaches. Ask the receiving engineer to define coverage before commissioning a scan. Useful areas can include the seating surface, mounting features, adjacent undertray edges and the transitions around the bumper corners.

CCF's [3D scan handover guide](https://ccfrp.com/3d-scan-data-carbon-fiber-parts-manufacturing/) explains the distinction between measured geometry and subsequent CAD work. In your RFQ, say whether the file is a point cloud, processed mesh or an engineered CAD model. File size and a smooth rendering do not establish that a manufacturing design exists.

Supply units, orientation, reference dimensions and a view showing scanned versus missing areas. Preserve the original data alongside edited exports. Hole filling and surface smoothing can help presentation, but the engineer must know where software has inferred a surface rather than measured it.

Do not specify a scan accuracy because another project used that number. The receiving team should define the required suitability for the interfaces, how it will be checked and what additional measurement is needed. Confirm accepted file types before paying for exports.

## Draw the mounting concept before freezing the styling

A front lip has a visible face and a hidden attachment problem. Increasing projection or changing an end shape can alter access, packaging and the space available for the return flange. A design review should examine a section through the bumper and proposed lip, not only a front-quarter render.

| Interface question | What the design review needs to resolve |
|---|---|
| Contact surface | Where the lip seats against the bumper and how the geometry is referenced |
| Fastening | Which existing or new attachment locations are proposed |
| Undertray | What is retained, removed or modified |
| Assembly | How a technician reaches and installs each fixing |
| Section joints | How separate pieces locate and how visible gaps are evaluated |
| Service | Whether the component can be removed without destroying adjacent parts |

If threaded inserts or bonded brackets are proposed, provide the mating parts and access constraints. CCF's [mounting-insert guidance](https://ccfrp.com/threaded-inserts-custom-carbon-fiber-parts/) is relevant here. The thread name alone does not determine the surrounding laminate, retention method or tightening specification. Those require the responsible designer's review.

Do not promise track performance from the shape. If the product is intended to carry defined aerodynamic loads, the engineering and validation scope must address them. A cosmetic prototype fitted to a stationary car is not aerodynamic performance evidence.

## Specify the carbon construction and finish as separate decisions

“Full carbon, gloss” leaves important questions unanswered. Describe the desired visible weave or chopped-fiber appearance, the surface finish and the intended construction. Ask the manufacturer to propose the laminate and process appropriate to the product requirements, including any backing or bonded features.

Prepreg, wet layup and infusion describe manufacturing inputs or routes; gloss and matte describe appearance. None of those labels alone establishes a completed part's fitment, weight or service capability. If mass matters, define the weighing scope and compare the delivered prototype against the agreed target.

At corners and sharp styling transitions, ask how the visible pattern will be evaluated. Use an approved physical reference or agreed photographs for appearance acceptance. A rendered image can conceal the weave movement and edge treatment that become apparent on a real molded component.

## Tooling price: identify what your payment buys

Request a development breakdown that separates geometry work, master preparation, production molds, trimming or checking fixtures, prototype production and revisions. Not every project requires the same items, and a quoted mold fee may not include them all.

The [CCF tooling ownership discussion](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-tooling-mold-ownership-questions/) raises another practical distinction: ownership, storage and permitted use need their own agreement. If you expect market exclusivity, future transfers or replacement tooling, describe those expectations before funding the project.

Also identify the reference revision used for the quote. If your team changes the lip's projection after approving the master, that is a different question from correcting a sample that does not match the approved geometry. Agree how each case is assessed rather than putting all later work under an undefined “free revisions” promise.

## The first physical sample should answer installation questions

Review the complete lip on the intended vehicle configuration. Inspect the corner seating, center contact, joint alignment, fixing access and undertray relationship before permanent attachment. Record modifications needed during the trial; a finished installation photograph can hide hours of adjustment.

Appearance can be accepted while an interface remains open. In that case, name the accepted surface reference and keep batch production on hold until the required fitment work is resolved. Do not let “looks good” become an instruction to manufacture inventory.

Send the reference photo, vehicle details, available geometry, proposed order volume and development questions through [CCF's OEM/ODM service](https://ccfrp.com/grow/oem-odm/). Ask first for the proposed development route and missing inputs. That discussion is more useful than comparing mold prices for five different interpretations of the same photograph.
