A customer wants carbon mirrors on a Porsche 992. You find two pairs with almost identical outside photographs. One is an overlay; the other replaces part of the original housing. Which belongs on your shelf?

**Stock the version your business can fit and support.** An overlay is worth considering when the customer wants an added carbon finish over a suitable existing shell. A replacement suits a job where the specified original cover will be removed and the workshop can carry out the documented installation. Neither deserves a bulk order until you have seen the attachment side and evaluated a sample on the intended housing.

The difference shows up in the installed edge, the labor bill and the first return. Those are more useful buying criteria than which photograph looks more expensive.

## What does your customer expect to change?

Start here, before discussing dry carbon or weave patterns. Does the customer want the painted shell covered, the shell replaced, or the whole mirror changed?

Those are three purchases. An overlay leaves the existing surface underneath. A replacement removes a defined component. A complete mirror assembly has a much wider scope; a cap quotation does not establish that glass, motors, wiring or a base are included.

For an online parts seller, that distinction belongs beside the price. A buyer who expects a replacement can reasonably be disappointed by a cover, even if the cover is well made. “Carbon mirror cap” alone leaves the customer guessing. CCF's [overlay and replacement comparison](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-overlay-vs-replacement-quotes/) explains the terminology if a supplier's product name is unclear.

| Your sales situation | Product worth evaluating | What could make it a poor choice |
|---|---|---|
| Customer wants carbon over an intact painted shell | An overlay with documented attachment requirements | Unwanted visible paint, an uneven perimeter or an unsuitable existing surface |
| Customer wants the original outer cover removed | A replacement for the verified housing | Unclear attachment features or an installation your shop cannot support |
| Customer has a damaged mirror | First establish which components are damaged | Treating a cosmetic cap as a repair for the housing beneath it |
| You sell across several markets | Separately identified, verified variants | Combining different housing or steering applications under one stock code |

This is a shortlist, not proof that both product types are available for every Porsche or BMW application.

## For overlays, buy the edge as well as the carbon face

The center of a glossy cover is easy to photograph. The perimeter tells you more about the result your customer will see.

On a sample, look around the glass-side edge, the lower housing and the tight corners. Does the cover sit evenly against the intended shell? Where does it stop? Is the original paint visible? A narrow strip that disappears on a black mirror may stand out on a lighter-colored car.

Request an installed close-up from those angles, plus the loose part's inner face. A distant three-quarter photograph of the car cannot answer the same questions. During the sample fitting, keep the attachment method reversible until the installer has assessed the seating under the supplier's procedure.

The existing surface matters too. Paint protection film, old adhesive, repaired paint or a previous cover changes what the overlay contacts. The workshop needs the attachment manufacturer's preparation and installation requirements for that surface. Adding more tape to pull an uneven shell into place hides a fitment problem rather than establishing a good sample.

For a retailer shipping to customers who arrange their own installation, the product page should show the perimeter clearly. That image may prevent more disappointed orders than another full-car photograph.

## Replacements need a labor quote before a retail price

A replacement can give the customer the structure they want, but “replacement” says little about the amount of work. Which cover comes off? What access is required? Are original components transferred? What attachment pieces remain on the housing?

There is a useful published example: [TECHART's 992 Sport Mirror Carbon instructions](https://www.techart.de/fileadmin/content/PDF/manuals/121_Carbon/992/092.121.851.099_TECHART_Carbon_Sport_Mirror_ENUS.pdf) describe a specific system involving mirror disassembly and base-frame work, with distinct LHD and RHD references. That is a different job from fitting a cosmetic cover. The instructions apply to that TECHART system; they do not establish how a CCF product is installed.

Put the installation document in front of the person who will do your job. Let the workshop price the actual removal, refitting and functional checks. If that information is unavailable, your customer quote still has an unresolved labor item.

The same applies to an online store's installation claims. “No drilling,” “no disassembly” and “fits all 992 models” are specific promises. They need evidence for the supplied reference. A generic mirror-cap video is a weak basis for any of them.

## Porsche 992: where a broad product listing breaks down

CCF's [992 mirror-cap catalog guide](https://ccfrp.com/porsche-992-carbon-fiber-mirror-caps-replacement-rhd-and-housing-checks/) distinguishes replacement descriptions at items 69–70, an RHD-labeled item 71 and another housing entry at item 72. That gives a purchaser several references to investigate, rather than one universal “992 carbon mirrors” product.

Keep the selected catalog edition and item with the supplier-confirmed SKU. The item number helps locate the picture; the accepted product reference makes the reorder traceable. Neighboring rows do not automatically share the RHD label or replacement structure.

For a customer's car, identify the model year, exact model, actual steering position and current housing. Photograph both mirrors. A previous accident replacement is relevant because the new cap must meet the housing now fitted to the car.

The [Porsche catalog page](https://ccfrp.com/vehicles/porsche/) separates model families and generations. Use the appropriate reference instead of carrying a 718 or a confirmed 992 application across to another model by appearance.

LHD/RHD also needs to stay separate from left/right. For example, an RHD car can need a vehicle-left cap. “Driver side” is ambiguous across markets. Write the side from the seated position facing forward, and state whether the quoted price buys one side or a pair.

## Inspect the attachment side before paying for the finish

Ask for the construction of the exact SKU. A visible carbon face may be combined with backing or a bonded carrier. That is information to evaluate, not something the phrase “real carbon” resolves.

For a replacement sample, inspect the tabs, seating edges and attachment structure with the installer. For an overlay, inspect its contact surfaces and perimeter. A gloss-versus-matte choice comes after those basics.

Then assess the pair together under comparable lighting. Look at the visible weave, corner transitions, wrapped edges and surface finish. If the buyer already has carbon trim, compare an actual sample or agreed reference. A 2x2 twill description alone does not guarantee that unrelated pieces will look identical.

CCF's [wholesale sample inspection guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-sample-inspection-wholesale-order/) provides a starting framework. For this purchase, the useful result is a sample record tied to a particular housing and product reference, with photographs your sales and installation teams can use.

## The cheaper pair can produce the weaker margin

Compare the amount left after serving the customer, including the installation work you include in the sale, separately purchased materials and the handling of a wrong-variant order. Use your own labor rates and actual supplier quotes. There is no dependable universal price difference between overlays and replacements.

Small carbon parts also create a particular after-sales question: can you replace one damaged side? A pair arriving as two separate pieces does not mean the supplier sells them individually. Establish spare-side availability and revision matching before advertising that service.

For your first shipment, favor the verified housing application for which you have customer demand. Adding gloss, matte, multiple steering variants and another installation structure can quickly turn one mirror-cap idea into several slow-moving SKUs. Order those alternatives when you have a reason to carry them.

A useful inquiry to [CCF wholesale](https://ccfrp.com/grow/wholesale/) is specific:

> Porsche 992 [model/year], actual [LHD/RHD], current housing photos attached. We need [overlay/replacement] in [finish], [quantity] complete left/right pairs for [destination]. Please identify the matching reference, supplied components, installation document and sample terms. Can a matching single side be supplied later?

With those answers and an evaluated sample, your team can explain what the customer is buying, price the work and recognize the same product on a reorder. That is the point at which a mirror-cap reference earns a place in stock.
