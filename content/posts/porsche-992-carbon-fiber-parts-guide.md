A Porsche 992 carbon fiber parts quote can look complete while leaving out the components that make the installation possible. A rear-wing photograph may show a blade, supports, base and engine-cover panel, while the price covers only the blade. A front lip may have the right general outline but belong to another bumper version.

**Start a 992 order with the exact car and the complete assembly you intend to install.** Then compare construction, finish and price. For a tuning shop or distributor, that sequence is more useful than selecting a package from a complete-car photograph and discovering the missing pieces after delivery.

This guide covers product selection and supplier questions for overseas B2B buyers. It separates catalog evidence from assumptions about fitment, so your customer offer describes a job you can actually price.

## “992” is a generation, not a complete application

Record the full model designation, production details, body style and current exterior configuration. Carrera, GTS, Turbo, Turbo S and GT models should not be collapsed into one purchasing field. Coupe, Cabriolet and Targa are also meaningful distinctions when the part interfaces with bodywork or rear assemblies.

The 992.1/992.2 distinction matters, but it is still only part of the identification. Registration year can be misleading across markets and model rollouts. Use vehicle-specific production information and photographs of the actual bumper or mounting assembly.

CCF's [Porsche catalogs](https://ccfrp.com/vehicles/porsche/) separate general 992, 992.1/992.2, Turbo and Turbo S inquiry routes. Those categories help find references. They do not prove that a product in one group fits every car in it, or that nothing can ever be shared between groups.

For a GT3 or GT3 RS inquiry, request the application directly. A GT-style appearance on a Carrera does not establish GT-model compatibility, and a conversion photograph does not identify all the changed components. Keep the original vehicle and its present bodywork visible in the inquiry.

## Choose the product around the customer's job

The following are product families to evaluate, not a ranking of best-selling Porsche accessories. Your customer inquiries and installation capability should determine which become stock.

| Product family | What the buyer is selecting | What can expand the order |
|---|---|---|
| Front lip and corner pieces | Lower-bumper profile and visible carbon treatment | Different bumper, separate vent trim, mounting parts |
| Side skirts or sill extensions | Side profile and perimeter finish | Body-specific version, separate left/right pieces |
| Rear diffuser or lower trim | Rear-bumper treatment | Exhaust compatibility, corners, surrounds and brackets |
| Rear wing or spoiler assembly | Wing surface or a larger rear conversion | Supports, base, engine-cover components and hardware |
| Mirror caps | Carbon cover or replacement housing component | Steering configuration, housing version and installation work |
| Intake and other trim | A selected accent at a defined location | Separate mounting or surrounding pieces |

For a lip-and-skirt order, do not automatically include a rear conversion because it appears in the same styling photograph. Quote the customer's intended stage of the build. That keeps optional work visible without turning an initial inquiry into an undefined complete kit.

## Front lips: 992.1 and 992.2 have separate catalog entries

CCF's [992.1 versus 992.2 front-lip guide](https://ccfrp.com/porsche-992-1-vs-992-2-carbon-fiber-front-lips-how-to-choose-the-right-version/) identifies a 992.1 lip at item 1, a 992.2 lip at item 2 and separate 992.2 vent trim at item 3 in the combined catalog. The practical buying consequence is straightforward: price the chosen lip and any vent trim separately unless the quotation expressly bundles them.

If a customer asks for the newer look on an earlier car, distinguish a lip for the existing bumper from a wider bumper conversion. Ordering item 2 does not, by itself, answer what else would be required.

Review the lip's full-width underside, corner returns and attachment edge against the bumper fitted to the car. Previous repairs, replaced trim and old splitter holes deserve photographs. A front-on styling view hides much of that interface.

There are functional differences to consider as well. Porsche's [2025 911 introduction](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en_US/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36337.html) describes model-specific front fascia changes and active air flaps on the Carrera GTS. That is a reason to identify the actual front assembly and equipment. It is not evidence that an aftermarket lip or vent cover preserves their operation.

For cars with relevant moving components or assistance sensors, have the supplier and installer review the product's relationship to them. Keep any unverified function out of the customer promise.

## Side skirts: identify the part beneath the marketing name

“Carbon side skirts” can refer to extensions, covers or replacement sections. Obtain a loose-part view showing the underside and both ends, then establish which original components remain on the vehicle.

For a pair, the quotation should identify vehicle-left and vehicle-right contents and the body application. Review the end profiles, seating surfaces and attachment method with the installer. Long parts can look almost identical in a small catalog image while meeting the car differently at their ends.

For wholesale stock, use separate internal codes for verified variants. Avoid putting unrelated bodies or styling references into a single inventory line because the supplier calls them all 992 skirts. When the outer carton is opened, the inner labels must still identify the components.

## Rear wings: compare the assembly, not just the blade

This is one of the clearest reasons to resist headline-price comparisons. CCF's [992 rear-wing assembly guide](https://ccfrp.com/porsche-992-carbon-fiber-rear-wings-what-should-be-included-in-the-assembly/) distinguishes wing and spoiler entries from combinations naming bases, engine-cover pieces and brackets. Its item 24 description names a wing, bracket, engine-cover plate and base.

That does not establish the hardware count or the installation procedure. It does establish that a blade-only price and a price covering those named components are different offers.

For your comparison, separate five lines: wing surface, supports, base, cover panel and fasteners. Mark each as supplied, retained, separately required or unresolved. Obtain the missing component's actual price before concluding that the lower wing price saves money.

Then review how the proposed assembly relates to the car's original rear mechanism. A photograph with the spoiler in one position cannot demonstrate movement, clearances or retained operation. The word “assembly” also does not answer those questions.

Have the installer inspect the applicable instructions before agreeing a fixed labor quote. A conversion involving a base and cover panel is a different scope from adding a decorative surface. Do not promise a particular aerodynamic benefit or road approval from the shape alone.

## Turbo and Turbo S: match the rear bumper and exhaust

Use the full model and the exhaust actually fitted. If the owner has changed the exhaust, include its reference and photographs showing tip shape, position and projection. A diffuser opening must be assessed against that combination.

CCF's [992 and Turbo S fitment resource](https://ccfrp.com/porsche-992-turbo-s-carbon-fiber-fitment/) explains why each component needs its own application review. A front lip accepted for a vehicle does not approve a skirt or rear section from another catalog group.

For a lower-rear assembly, identify center and corner pieces, surrounding trim and mounting parts. Ask which sections are intended to be installed together. If the quote depends on the customer retaining a specific original component, keep it on the installation record.

The installer also needs the relevant heat and clearance requirements for the actual exhaust and composite part. No general tip diameter or cold gap validates every combination. If a required component remains unidentified, keep the customer total provisional rather than entering a zero-cost line.

## Mirror caps: a smaller shipment still needs a precise variant

Mirror caps can be a useful inquiry when a customer wants a smaller exterior change, but their size does not make them universal. A replacement housing component and an overlay have different installation interfaces.

CCF's [992 mirror-cap guide](https://ccfrp.com/porsche-992-carbon-fiber-mirror-caps-replacement-rhd-and-housing-checks/) flags replacement descriptions and an explicit RHD entry. Retain the selected reference, actual steering position and current housing in the order. Do not infer the application of every neighboring catalog entry from one RHD label.

Specify vehicle-left, vehicle-right or a complete pair separately from LHD/RHD. ## Materials and finish: ask what the selected part contains

Dry carbon, visible weave and gloss are three different descriptions. They should not be used as substitutes for one another in a supplier comparison.

Ask for the construction of the exact reference, including any backing, bonded carrier or attachment structure. A carbon face does not describe the complete laminate. If the supplier claims a weight saving, establish the scope of the comparison: bare panel or complete assembly, with which supports and hardware.

For a matched exterior package, inspect the proposed appearance across parts under comparable lighting. Look at the weave around corners, visible edges, surface finish and any painted sections. A pattern name cannot guarantee an exact match to carbon already on the customer's car.

Gloss or matte alone also says nothing about long-term surface performance. If durability is important to the customer's requirements, request relevant product and coating information. Avoid turning a generic “UV resistant” label into a specific warranty promise.

## Evaluate a supplier through a sample order you can inspect

A good first sample request names the car, product reference and installation objective. It also includes the supplied component list. Without that list, your receiving team can admire a flawless wing surface while overlooking a missing base needed for the job.

Photograph labels, packaging, attachment features and visible finish before installation. Have the workshop inspect the fit using the applicable instructions. Record any extra parts or adjustments required, along with the exact vehicle configuration used for the review.

CCF's [sample inspection guide](https://ccfrp.com/carbon-fiber-sample-inspection-wholesale-order/) provides a general framework. Add the product-specific questions here: front-lip version, full wing contents, retained rear mechanism or mirror housing. A successful mirror-cap sample does not establish how a supplier's rear conversion will fit.

If the sample needs trimming or force to locate, send the original interface evidence before alteration. For reorders, retain the accepted reference and ask whether tooling, construction, mounting or supplied contents changed. The most attractive sample photograph is not a substitute for an identifiable production version.

## Request the Porsche 992 catalog and a usable wholesale quote

Send a separate line for each product, with the catalog edition, reference image and confirmed part number when available. Include the exact 992 model, body, production details, modifications, finish, quantity and destination.

For a mixed order, obtain the proposed packing and actual carton dimensions before finalizing freight. A large wing or cover assembly should not be assigned the same shipping allowance as a mirror-cap pair. Compare goods price, additional installation pieces, shipping scope and workshop cost on the same basis.

Use [CCF wholesale supply](https://ccfrp.com/grow/wholesale/) to request the appropriate catalog and product-specific prices. Ask for sample terms and availability for the actual shortlist; avoid applying one assumed MOQ or lead time to every Porsche product. If the request changes geometry or creates a new combination, take it through [OEM/ODM review](https://ccfrp.com/grow/oem-odm/) before treating it as existing catalog supply.

A concise inquiry might read:

> Porsche 992 [exact model, body and production details]. Current bumper, exhaust and rear assembly photos attached. Requested references: [separate lines and quantities]. Please identify supplied versus retained components, construction, finish, installation information, sample terms and packing for delivery to [destination].

## Porsche 992 buyer questions

### Will a 992.2 front lip fit a 992.1 car?

The combined CCF catalog has distinct front-lip references. Select by the actual bumper and obtain the application's supported scope. A request for newer styling does not establish a lip-only conversion.

### Can Carrera and Turbo S carbon parts be combined?

Only where each selected reference supports the actual car and its current interfaces. The catalog group alone does not prove either universal compatibility or universal incompatibility.

### Does a rear-wing set include the base and engine-cover panel?

It depends on the selected entry and quotation. Compare the named components individually, and obtain hardware and retained-part details separately.

### Which Porsche 992 carbon fiber parts should a dealer stock first?

Start with verified references supported by customer demand and installation capability. Larger rear assemblies and unresolved conversions can remain quotation-led until the component scope, packing and after-sales work are understood.
